Business leaders are under growing pressure to deliver improved productivity, more consistent employee experiences and faster returns on their investments in AI.
Yet even as organisations modernise their tools, the digital workplace itself is becoming harder to manage. Hybrid work has expanded the number of devices, applications and services employees depend on each day, with operating models that were built for simpler, office-based environments being stretched to their limit.
The result is a widening performance gap. While leaders aim to create workplaces that feel seamless and intuitive, many employees still face friction the moment something goes wrong. They’re forced to switch between support channels, often dealing with frustrating wait times and repeated problems. At the same time, IT teams spend more energy navigating their fragmented infrastructure than driving strategic improvement.
To close this gap, organisations are exploring a new maturity model for support known as invisible IT. The approach aims to predict and resolve most IT issues quietly in the background, long before they disrupt employees.
As AI becomes embedded across devices and applications, invisible IT is emerging as one of the most effective ways to reduce friction, maintain stability and give people the freedom to focus on high-value work.

## Fragmented systems are limiting digital workplace performance
This lack of connection limits visibility, introduces unnecessary steps and erodes the performance gains that leaders expect from their digital investments.
Many organisations also still rely on support structures designed for an era when work happened mostly in the office. These models depend on users noticing an issue, reporting it and waiting for it to be routed through the support queue.
**Reactive workflows create hidden costs**
This reactive pattern creates three consequences:
1. Slower recovery times: early warning signs go unnoticed, making issues harder to diagnose.
2. Rising operational noise: teams spend more time firefighting, less time improving the environment.
3. Inconsistent employee experiences: individuals face avoidable interruptions that break focus and reduce engagement.
**Limited personalisation compounds the problem**
With only a minority of organisations tailoring assistance to real usage patterns, issues often recur and troubleshooting takes longer than it should.
IT teams can’t move quickly enough to meet modern expectations. This slows transformation, undermines confidence in new technologies and makes it harder for organisations to deliver the productivity gains leaders are looking for.

## What invisible IT looks like in practice
Instead of reacting to issues after productivity has already taken a hit, IT teams can use continuous telemetry, AI-driven insights and automation to stabilise systems before problems surface. The result is a workplace that feels more predictable and consistent for employees, a place where they can concentrate on doing their best work.
**Predictive and proactive operations**
Invisible IT uses AI to analyse device health, application behaviour and performance indicators across the environment. When early signs of degradation appear, automated actions can resolve the issue or route it to the right engineer with full context.
Lenovo’s pilot programmes show what this looks like:
- 40% of issues were resolved before a ticket is created
- Support costs were reduced by 30%
- Onboarding times improved by 50%
These gains demonstrate the value of shifting from user-reported incidents to signal-driven operations.
**Support that adapts to how people actually work**
Invisible IT goes beyond device telemetry. It uses AI-generated personas to understand how individuals and teams actually use tools and move through their workday. Support then adapts automatically based on real usage patterns.
This creates:
- More consistent experiences across hybrid and distributed teams
- Faster issue resolution tailored to the user’s environment
- Fewer recurring incidents caused by generic troubleshooting workflows
**Elevating IT teams**
Invisible IT strengthens engineering capability rather than shrinking it. Automation handles routine tasks, giving IT teams more time to focus on improvement, prevention and strategic initiatives.
This aligns with Lenovo’s findings that only a small minority of leaders expect workforce reductions as automation increases, with most planning to redirect staff toward higher-value responsibilities. By augmenting teams with AI-driven insight, organisations build a support model that scales more effectively than traditional workflows.

## What IT leaders should prioritise next
IT leaders can accelerate their move toward proactive, high-performing operations by prioritising three areas:
**1. Build an integrated data foundation**
Predictive support only works when information from devices, applications and support channels come together in a consistent and connected way. Leaders should focus on:
- Consolidating endpoint, application and support data into unified systems
- Reducing silos that obscure early warning signals
- Establishing shared taxonomies so AI can classify issues more accurately
**2. Strengthen team capability for AI-enabled operations**
As automation takes on routine tasks, IT roles shift toward interpreting signals, refining workflows and improving overall experience. Business leaders can enable this shift by:
- Training teams to understand AI-generated insights and trust machine-identified patterns
- Redesigning support workflows so intervention happens earlier in the lifecycle
- Giving engineers time and space to focus on prevention rather than queue-based resolution
**3. Use experienced partners to accelerate maturity**
For many organisations the challenge is capacity. Fragmented environments, AI skills gaps and limited time make it difficult to modernise support models alone. Partners can help by:
- Integrating data pipelines and unifying telemetry.
- Testing predictive models and validating automation in real environments.
- Embedding new ways of working that reduce noise and improve stability.
## A clearer path to digital workplace performance
Invisible IT gives organisations a more predictable, resilient path to improving productivity and employee experience.
By shifting from user-reported issues to signal-driven insight, leaders gain earlier visibility into risks and greater control over how disruptions are managed; employees face fewer interruptions; IT teams spend less time on rote tasks; and the organisation benefits from a workplace that performs consistently, even as it grows in scale and complexity.
