## Formative years
Jensen co-founded [NVIDIA](https://aimagazine.com/company/nvidia) in 1993 and has led it through multiple platform shifts, from graphics acceleration to high performance computing and now AI-first infrastructure.
In NVIDIA’s early years, the company came close to collapse after several unsuccessful product launches – an experience he has since cited as formative in shaping his disciplined approach to execution and long-term risk.
That resilience, combined with deep technical fluency, has allowed him to lead NVIDIA through repeated reinvention while maintaining clear direction as the company evolved into an AI-first business.
What separates Jensen from many technology CEOs is continuity.
He has spent decades building both the hardware roadmap and the software ecosystem required to make accelerated computing useful at scale.
That long view also shows in how he describes AI’s impact on the industry.
At the recent CES 2026 in Las Vegas, he argued that “the computer industry is being reinvented”, linking the AI boom to a broader modernisation cycle across computing.

## Flying the flag for innovation
At CES, Jensen used his keynote stage to [outline NVIDIA’s next AI moves](https://aimagazine.com/news/inside-jensen-huangs-next-ai-moves-as-nvidia-ceo), stressing that the company’s new processors are already in “full production”.
He also claimed the new generation delivers “five times the artificial intelligence computing” of the last generation when used for chatbots and other AI tools.
A central pillar of that roadmap is the Rubin platform, designed to scale from individual servers into larger configurations.
Jensen explained: "This is how we were able to deliver such a gigantic step up in performance, even though we have 1.6 times the number of transistors."
He also focused heavily on inference – the real-time side of AI – where competition is intensifying.
NVIDIA’s answer includes “context memory storage”, aimed at sustaining coherence over longer interactions and more complex prompts.
This matters because influence in AI is increasingly defined not just by training ever-larger models, but by running them efficiently and reliably at scale.
Jensen’s leadership also shows in NVIDIA’s push beyond processors towards autonomous systems.
Introducing Alpamayo, an AI model built for autonomous vehicles, he said: "Not only do we open-source the models, we also open-source the data that we use to train those models, because only in that way can you truly trust how the models came to be."

## Prioritising AI
When Jensen speaks, audiences listen.
Taking to the stage of at the [World Economic Forum's Annual Meeting 2026 in Davos](https://aimagazine.com/news/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-wef-davos-2026), he positioned AI as a national priority.
"AI is infrastructure and there’s not one country in the world I can’t imagine that you need to have AI as part of your infrastructure," Jensen asserted.
“Because every country has its electricity, you have your roads – you should have AI as part of your infrastructure.”
Ultimately, Jensen’s leadership at NVIDIA has defined how AI hardware scales, how infrastructure is conceived and how the technology is being prioritised on a national and global level.
Over the coming years, his influence and decision-making will continue to define the direction of artificial intelligence.
